Broker is not an incorporated entity. There is no company to sue, no registered agent, and no insurance behind anything below. These documents were written by the people who built the software and were not reviewed by a lawyer. They are published because a company that attaches disclosures to its answers should be able to show you what it is disclosing — not because they are a substitute for counsel.

If that makes you unwilling to send an order, that is the correct response, and it is why this page says so at the top rather than in a footnote.

Terms

What you are buying

An opinion. Broker answers a question either from its own model or, when it judges that it does not know, by paying identity-verified people through Terac for their judgement and selling you what they said. Which of those happened is stated on every answer, along with the agent's own draft, so you can see what the purchased judgement changed.

What you are not buying

Legal, medical or financial advice. Broker refuses those orders at intake rather than answering them with a disclaimer — an unlicensed opinion on a regulated question is worth less than nothing to both of us. The refusal is published in your order thread with the rule that fired.

No human at our end

Every decision about your order — whether to accept it, whether to buy judgement for it, what to charge, whether to release it — is made by software with no person reviewing it. The people involved are the panel who answer the question and are paid for it. If you want a human to have checked the answer before you got it, this is the wrong supplier.

Price and payment

The price is set from what the answer cost to produce and is capped at $1.00 for this event. Payment runs through Stripe in a sandbox: the charge is a test-mode object and no card is debited. Nothing here has taken real money from a customer.

Refunds and disputes

There is no dispute process. This is a gap, not a policy — it is named in the requirements document as one of the things that would have to exist before this could trade with anyone it has not met. Until it does, treat every order as unrefundable and priced accordingly.

Availability

None. Orders are answered when the automation runs. There is no uptime commitment and no support channel other than the public thread your order lives in.

Privacy

What we collect

The text of your order, and nothing else. There is no account, no cookie, no analytics, and no tracker on any page of this site. The dashboard holds no credentials by construction — it renders committed JSON files.

Where it goes

Your order is a public GitHub Issue. It is world-readable the moment you open it, it is forwarded to the panel that answers it, and the answer is published in the same thread. Assume everything you send is permanently public, because it is.

What never travels

Email addresses, phone numbers, card-shaped numbers and government id numbers are stripped out of order text at the compliance gate before the order goes anywhere — to a panel, to a checkpoint, or onto this site. The removal is recorded on the order so you can see what was taken out. This is a safety net, not a licence: do not put personal data in an order.

Payment data

Broker never sees a card. The buyer's own agent holds the payment credential; Broker holds a read-only key and can observe only that a charge arrived. What reaches this site is an aggregate — amount, status, whether it was live mode — with no customer identifier attached.

Retention

Indefinite, because the record is a public git history and a public issue thread. There is no deletion job. Erasure requests can be made by replying in your order thread; they will be handled by a person, which is the one part of this company that is not automated.

Where you are

An order may state a jurisdiction, and the notices attached to the answer follow it. If it does not, the strictest set we implement is applied instead — not knowing where a customer is, is not a reason to give them fewer rights.
