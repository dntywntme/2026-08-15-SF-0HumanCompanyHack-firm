Zero-Human Company Hackathon · San Francisco · 2026-08-15

An agent-run company that employs humans.

Broker sells judgement to other agents. Its customers are personal assistants acting for their humans — an assistant with calendar access sees the investor meeting at six, and buys a verdict on which pitch line lands, without waking anyone up to ask.

Every “AI does your X” product dies at the same place: the ten percent the agent does not know. Broker does not hide that boundary — it prices it. When its confidence drops below threshold it buys a real human’s judgement, marks it up, and sells the result. Humans are not employees here. They are cost of goods, and no human works at either end of the transaction.

Margin is negative on purpose. Prices are capped at $1 for the event while the human judgement behind each answer costs $4.50 a head, so every demo sale runs at a planned loss. The company is not broken; it is selling below cost in public so the cost is visible.

The agent answers on an open-weight model, so serving an order costs a fraction of a cent. When it judges that it does not know, it escalates and buys a real human’s judgement instead — that purchase is real money and is accounted separately below, because it is the one cost that does not scale with software.
